3. Ground-state fluctuations and available work
----------------------------------------------

For H = p^2/(2M) + M*omega^2*q^2/2 and [q,p]=i*hbar,

  H = hbar*omega*(a_dagger*a + 1/2),
  <N>_vac = 0, E0 = hbar*omega/2,
  <q^2>_vac = hbar/(2*M*omega), Var(H)_vac = 0.

Coordinate fluctuations coexist with definite total energy and zero
occupation relative to that oscillator's annihilation operator. A claimed
virtual-pair population needs a detector, field/state, resolution, number
observable and protocol. Perturbative internal propagators supply amplitudes
rather than a universal census of temporary particles.

For a system initially in its ground state and a cyclic unitary U,

  W_out = E0 - <0|U_dagger*H*U|0> <= 0.

The lower spectral bound proves the inequality. Its premises name a ground
state, the same initial/final Hamiltonian, closed unitary evolution, and a
complete accounted system. Driven devices, heat gradients, prepared states,
measurements and feedback introduce additional resources. The theorem's
premises must be checked before applying the conclusion to a device.

For finite diagonal states, diagonal_ergotropy pairs descending populations
with ascending energies to compute the passive energy. A population-inverted
two-level state has extractable work; a ground-state population has zero
ergotropy. The work calculation permits arbitrary cyclic unitaries and a
finite truncation. Restricted controls can extract less work. Passivity of
one copy and complete passivity under arbitrary copies are distinct claims.

Adding C*I to a nongravitational Hamiltonian changes a global phase.

constructed only from those observables has zero information about C.

5. Ordered quantum noise supplies the cheapest physical distinction
------------------------------------------------------------------

Use Sqq(omega)=integral dt exp(+i*omega*t)<q(t)q(0)>.
For a thermal harmonic oscillator, q0^2=hbar/(2*M*omega0),

  Sqq(omega) = 2*pi*q0^2 * [
      (n_B+1)*delta(omega-omega0) + n_B*delta(omega+omega0)],
  n_B = 1/expm1(hbar*omega0/(k_B*T)).

At T=0 the positive-frequency absorption line survives and the
negative-frequency emission line vanishes. Detailed balance gives
Sqq(-omega0)/Sqq(+omega0)=exp(-hbar*omega0/(k_B*T)). Line coefficients have
units m^2; the angular-frequency spectrum has units m^2*s. The integral
of Sqq/(2*pi) gives <q^2>. The symmetrized spectrum splits the two lines
equally and cannot alone determine a detector's excitation rate.

A real stationary scalar classical process has an even autocorrelation and
a symmetric auto-spectrum. Replacing the ordered quantum spectrum with a
classical zero-point waveform therefore changes emission/absorption unless
an additional response/backaction construction repairs the model. A
two-level detector at low temperature provides a decisive check. Classical
dephasing models retain their own stated regime of usefulness.

6. Boundary mechanics, memory and coherence
------------------------------------------

Perfect-conductor zero-temperature planar plates have

  U_C(a) = -pi^2*hbar*c*A/(720*a^3),
  P_C(a) = -pi^2*hbar*c/(240*a^4), F=-dU_C/da.

At a=100 nm and A=1 um^2, U_C is about -4.33e-19 J and P_C about -13 Pa.
Approach releases a finite interaction-energy change. Resetting the same
parameters costs the same work in a conservative ideal cycle. Switching
conductivity, geometry, charges, temperature or phase adds control channels.
The full cycle must restore each device, store, controller and relevant
reservoir condition before a repeatable-resource claim can be assessed.

Let U_eff(x)=k*x^2/2+U_C(d+x), where x increases the gap. A supported
equilibrium satisfies U_eff'=0. Local linear stability requires
k_eff=U_eff''>0, M>0 and positive dissipation. Attractive ideal Casimir
curvature is negative: U_C''=-12*C_area/(d+x)^5 for U_C=-C_area/(d+x)^3.
Increasing attraction can soften the support or cause pull-in. Stability
at a measured point supplies a local result; a singular potential and
large excursions require separate analysis.

In the classical thermal harmonic regime, <dx^2>=k_B*T/k_eff. Softening
increases variance at fixed temperature. The independent displacement-to-
frequency calibration G=domega_q/dx gives S_frequency=G^2*S_x only when
other channels and cross-correlations are correctly handled. A large static
force alone determines neither that spectrum nor a coherence benefit.

For zero-mean classical Gaussian frequency noise and ideal switching y(t),

  W_y(t)=exp[-t/(2*T1) - (1/2)*integral ds ds' y(s)y(s') C_omega(s-s')].

C_omega has units s^-2; the exponent is dimensionless. Prepared-state
coherence can be defined as 2*abs(rho_01). A signed sine-wave amplitude is
a different observable. Ramsey and echo predictions require the declared
pulse sequence, calibrated coupling, physical bath and appropriate quantum
or classical approximation. Matched cooldowns, temperature, loss, T1,
drive leakage and drift adjudicate an observed coherence advantage.

For exponential mechanical memory F_j obeying

  M*xddot+eta*xdot+k*x+sum_j F_j=F_drive,
  Fdot_j=-F_j/tau_j+b_j*xdot,
  E=M*xdot^2/2+k*x^2/2+sum_j F_j^2/(2*b_j),
  Edot=F_drive*xdot-eta*xdot^2-sum_j F_j^2/(b_j*tau_j).

M,k,b_j,tau_j>0 and eta>=0 define nonnegative storage and dissipation.
