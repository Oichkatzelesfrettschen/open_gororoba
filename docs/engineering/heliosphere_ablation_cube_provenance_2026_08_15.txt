heliosphere ablation cube provenance
====================================

last_verified: 2026-08-15
scope: data/output/heliosphere/ablations
evidence_class: measured

what is in the directory
------------------------

379 files, 3.43 GiB, git-lfs tracked. Two schema families, and the split is
exact -- every file belongs to one or the other, and the two differ by three
orders of magnitude in size.

Feature cubes carry the `HeliosphereFeatureRow` header
`window_name,mission,product,year,doy,hour,r_au,lat_deg,lon_deg,density_cm3,...`
declared in `crates/data_core/src/heliosphere_feature_cube.rs`. There are 37 of
them and they hold 3.39 GiB, so they are effectively the whole directory. They
are inputs: one row per (mission, product, year, doy, hour), whose twelve
dynamic channels `dynamic_signal_channels` hands to the Cayley-Dickson
embedding.

Analysis results carry headers like
`r_center_au,lat_center_deg,mean_associator,median_associator,max_associator`
or `mission,block_size_K,base_mean,block_null_mean,...`. They run 4 to 8 KB
each. These are the outputs, and the pairing with their cube is visible in the
names: `messenger_mercury_2011w12.csv` at 533 MB against
`messenger_mercury_cd_32d.csv` at 4 KB, `cassini_2000_2004.csv` at 220 MB
against `cassini_saturn_cd_32d.csv`, `ace_mag_2018.csv` at 228 MB against
`ace_2018_cd_32d.csv`.

The scientific evidence is therefore in the kilobyte files. The gigabytes are
the substrate they were computed from.

how a cube works into the multidimensional analysis
---------------------------------------------------

`HeliosphereFeatureRow::dynamic_signal_channels` returns twelve channels --
density_cm3, speed_kms, temperature_k, bx, by, bz, b_mag, crs_flux,
spectral_mean, spectral_peak, map_flux_mean, map_flux_std -- and deliberately
omits r_au, lat_deg and lon_deg, which arrive in three incompatible frames
across missions. Those twelve are embedded into a Cayley-Dickson algebra, at
D=32 for every result file here, and the associator is evaluated over triples.
The result files then bin by `r_center_au` and `lat_center_deg` and report the
mean, median and maximum associator per bin.

The MAG-only cubes -- Voyager, MESSENGER, Cassini, ACE -- populate only four of
those twelve channels, leaving density, speed, temperature, crs_flux and the
four spectral and map channels at NaN. A cube of that shape exercises the
magnetic sector of the embedding and nothing else.

reproducibility, measured
-------------------------

`voyager hapi-ingest` converts a CDAWeb HAPI CSV into this schema. It is
deterministic. Regenerating `ace_mag_2018.csv` from the source already on disk
reproduces the stored file byte for byte:

  voyager hapi-ingest --spacecraft ACE \
    --input data/external/hapi_downloads/ac_h0_mfi_2018.csv \
    --out-csv <path> --col-dist=-1

  1961558 records, 239013307 bytes, sha256 e62c58bbbaa2c72e...
  identical to the stored ace_mag_2018.csv by cmp and by sha256.

Two properties fall out of that measurement. The conversion needs no network,
because `data/external/` already holds the sources. And the cube is 2.35 times
larger than the source it derives from -- 228 MB from 97 MB -- because it
expands five numeric columns into twenty-one, seventeen of which are the
literal string NaN or a constant zero.

For every other cube the generation command is not recorded anywhere in the
repository. The sources exist -- data/external/hapi_downloads (891 MB),
voyager_mag48_cdf (488 MB), voyager_coho_hapi (8.7 MB), messenger (1.3 GB),
cassini (3.5 MB), ulysses (31 MB), mms (831 MB), pioneer (23 MB) -- but the
mapping from source to cube does not, and two cases show it is not a matter of
guessing the flags. The Cassini source carries only a timestamp, |B| and a
distance, while its cube carries bx, by and bz, so at least two inputs were
merged. The MESSENGER source reports distance as 4.9269345e+07 against a cube
value of 0.324, a kilometres-to-AU conversion `hapi-ingest` does not perform.

That is the finding: 3.25 GiB of derived intermediates whose derivation is
unrecorded. They are not reproducible artifacts in the sense the registry
means, and they are not raw data either. Recording the command per cube is the
work that turns them back into either one.

The registry agrees, by omission. `artifact_paths` in
`registry/canonical/control_plane.sqlite3` holds 3915 rows and **zero** of them
name anything under `data/output/heliosphere/ablations`, and
`registry/datasets.toml` declares no dataset there either. So the directory is
outside every provenance lane the repository operates: not a declared dataset,
not a registered artifact, not cited by 278 of its 379 files. It is the largest
unaccounted-for object in `data/output` after `benchmark_scores.csv`.

containment
-----------

Filenames claiming completeness do not always deliver it, so containment was
measured on the full key set (product, year, doy, hour) rather than assumed.

  v1_mag48_full_2009_2025.csv    41031 keys, 0 absent from v1_complete_lifetime
  v1_full_lifetime_1979_2025.csv 131674 keys, 1759 absent from v1_complete_lifetime
  v1_complete_lifetime_1977_2025.csv                                143111 keys

So `v1_complete_lifetime` subsumes `v1_mag48_full` exactly and does not subsume
`v1_full_lifetime`, despite the names. The Voyager 2 family does not nest at
all: `v2_inner_1_60au.csv` covers 1977 through 1988, which
`v2_complete_lifetime.csv` does not carry.

removed
-------

Two files, 563 MB, each on a measurement rather than an inference.

  ace_mag_2018.csv          228 MB  regenerated byte-identical, command above
  v1_mag48_full_2009_2025.csv 335 MB  every key present in v1_complete_lifetime

retained
--------

Everything else, including all 35 remaining feature cubes. Each is either cited
by name in the registry, or has no recorded generation command and no verified
superset, which makes deletion irreversible in practice regardless of the
sources sitting in data/external.
