# CD associator as a magnetopause-crossing detector -- Staples THEMIS benchmark

_2026-07-16. Ground truth: Staples et al. 2020 THEMIS Magnetopause Crossings V2
(2007-2016), `data/external/crossing_lists/themis_mp_crossings_v2.txt`._

## Setup
- **Ground truth:** 9,490 THEMIS-A magnetopause crossings (of 29,141 total across probes
  a/b/c/d/e), at median 11.5 R_E (6.8-19.2), matched to 835 THEMIS-A FGM files by day.
- **Detector:** normalized (dimensionless) CD sedenion associator -- b_field=[Bx,By,Bz,|B|],
  4-sample sedenion stack, associator norm over 3 consecutive sedenions, divided by
  |a||b||c| to remove the degree-3 |B| homogeneity.
- **Task:** per-timepoint binary detection; positive = within +/-2 min of a catalogued
  crossing. **24.3M points, 617k positives (2.53% base rate).**
- **Baselines:** |dB/dt| (compression), field rotation angle, |B|, and a naive
  rotationxcompression product.

## Results
| detector | ROC-AUC | best-F1 |
|---|---|---|
| **CD associator (normalized)** | **0.826** | 0.136 |
| rotation angle | 0.820 | 0.138 |
| naive rotx|dB/dt| | 0.743 | -- |
| |dB/dt| (compression) | 0.706 | 0.091 |
| |B| | 0.302 | -- |

**Honest headline (CI-backed):** the associator is the best single scalar and its
edge over field-rotation angle is small but statistically decisive. The paired
moving-block bootstrap (E-235: 29,000-sample blocks matching the daily-file
autocorrelation scale, 200 paired resamples, ChaCha8 seed 42;
data/output/staples_roc_block_bootstrap.json) gives
AUC_assoc = 0.8260 [0.8186, 0.8329], AUC_rot = 0.8203 [0.8139, 0.8274], and
paired delta = +0.0056 [0.0049, 0.0067]; the delta CI excludes zero and
rotation wins in 0 of 200 resamples. The per-detector CIs overlap, which is
exactly why the PAIRED delta is the honest statistic: both detectors face the
same resampled days, so day-to-day difficulty variation cancels. The advantage
is real but modest on bulk detection; the sample-regime robustness below remains
the stronger differentiator. "Best single scalar" means best among the four
implemented baselines (rotation angle, |dB/dt|, |B|, rot x |dB/dt|).

## Where the associator actually wins: robustness across sample regimes
Positive TIME POINTS split at the median |dB/dt| among positives into
high-gradient and low-gradient SAMPLE REGIMES (per-point strata, deliberately
not called crossing subtypes: one crossing event can contribute points to both
strata, and event-level compressive/rotational classification needs integrated
per-event statistics this scorer does not compute):

| detector | high-gradient AUC | low-gradient AUC | **worst-case** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CD associator** | 0.898 | 0.754 | **0.754** |
| rotation angle | 0.889 | 0.752 | 0.752 |
| |dB/dt| | 0.867 | 0.545 | 0.545 |

**|dB/dt| collapses to 0.545 on low-gradient positives** -- the regime it cannot see.
Rotation angle does NOT collapse: it stays strong on both (0.889 high-gradient, 0.752
low-gradient), because most magnetopause crossings involve a field rotation. The
associator matches this robust rotation baseline on both regimes (0.898 / 0.754) and
edges ahead on BOTH -- the paired block bootstrap (E-235) puts every stratum delta above
zero: high-gradient +0.0093 [0.0083, 0.0104], low-gradient +0.0020 [0.0008, 0.0034], and
the worst-case-AUC (min over strata) delta +0.0020 [0.0008, 0.0034]. The associator
therefore strictly dominates the rotation baseline ON ROC-AUC across the selected
coordinates -- bulk, high-gradient, low-gradient, and the floor -- without the
|dB/dt|-style blind spot. Scope limits on that sentence: rotation retains the slightly
higher reported best-F1 (0.138 vs 0.136), so this is AUC dominance, not universal
detector dominance; and "best conventional" means best among the four implemented
baselines -- PVI, cumulative six-sample rotation, windowed magnetic shear, temporal
torsion, and wavelet energy remain unimplemented equal-information controls. The margins
stay modest -- the value is **robustness in one scalar with a uniformly positive AUC
edge**, not a large margin.
The bulk delta is insensitive to the block-length choice: half/double sweep
(14,500 / 29,000 / 58,000 samples) moves the 95% CI endpoints by less than 0.0002.

## Cluster-level inference and the simultaneous dominance certificate (E-237)
The flat CSV concatenates 834 daily files, so contiguous blocks can straddle file
boundaries; the file-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples of INTACT daily files, every
sample within each drawn file retained, exporter now emits file_id) is the valid
sampling-unit design for population inference and supersedes the E-235 block intervals
for that purpose. Results (data/output/staples_roc_cluster_bootstrap.json):
bulk delta +0.0056 [0.0047, 0.0065]; high-gradient +0.0093 [0.0083, 0.0103];
low-gradient +0.0020 [0.0008, 0.0032]; SIMULTANEOUS MARGIN
M = min(three paired deltas, recomputed inside every resample) = +0.0020
[0.0008, 0.0032] with 1 nonpositive draw in 2,000 (p_le_zero = 0.001) -- strict
componentwise AUC dominance certified as ONE proposition, not inferred from three
marginal intervals. Mean daily AUC (typical crossing day): assoc 0.8196, rot 0.8145,
delta +0.0051 [0.0040, 0.0064]. Tail proportions report as < 1/(B+1) when zero
nonpositive draws occur; Monte Carlo zeros are resolution statements, not exact.
Claims: C-1628 (Verified, deterministic benchmark dominance), C-1629 (Provisional,
population-level, pending probe replication and equal-information baselines),
C-1630 (Verified, coefficient census: 1848-term sparse cubic filter, e0 empty,
e8=168, others 120). The exact tensor census (E-238) identifies the detector as a
fixed sparse cubic filter over six samples -- the mechanism question is now whether
the CD coefficient pattern beats matched generic cubic filters, not whether more
samples are needed.

## Companion survey: cross-mission log-sigma CIs (E-236)
File-level bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples over daily files) on the selfsim survey's
per-mission mean log-sigma expose an instrument/regime confound the point estimates hid:
the three 1-AU missions separate with non-overlapping CIs -- ACE 1.52 [1.45, 1.59],
Cluster 2.27 [1.95, 2.59], THEMIS 3.23 [2.94, 3.49] -- so same-distance spread exceeds
any distance trend, and the PSP magnetometer point (3.64 [2.99, 4.29]) rests on 2 files.
A heliocentric-distance reading of log-sigma requires instrument and plasma-regime
covariates before it supports any claim.

## Caveats (what would raise the numbers)
- Absolute F1 ~0.14 is low because this is raw per-timepoint, single-threshold, single-feature
  detection with no orbit-phase gating, no temporal smoothing, no ML. The repo's own pipeline
  (windowed descriptors + logistic regression + Takens embedding) is expected to score much
  higher; this measures raw discriminative power of one feature.
- +/-2 min label window and the high/low-gradient median split are design choices; the
  qualitative ordering (associator >= rotation > |dB/dt| >> |B|, associator best worst-case) is
  stable to reasonable variations.
- THEMIS-A only; probes c/d/e (18k more crossings) not yet included.
