# Executed next experiments — 2026-09-10

This report records model experiments integrating the nuisance-orthogonal identifiability framework with optical and Casimir parameterizations taken from open_gororoba commit `e036a72b87b9915847cde2ed1908f319cc67eeca`.

The calculations are not physical detections. The multilayer extension uses recursive TE/TM Fresnel reflection coefficients at imaginary frequency and the repository Drude / Drude-Lorentz parameters for Au, SiO2, Al2O3, Si, WO3, and WO3-x.

## Multilayer validation

For Au(20 nm) / SiO2(50 nm) / Al2O3(50 nm) / Si against Au at `d = 200 nm`, the zero-temperature pressure converged from `-0.471385 Pa` at quadrature order 24 to `-0.470936 Pa` at order 80, or `0.579558` times the ideal perfect-conductor pressure.

The recursive multilayer implementation passed three limiting-case checks over 100–400 nm: a vanishing silica film approached the bare-Si half-space result with relative error below `2.4e-8`; a 10 um silica film approached the silica half-space result within `5.5e-6` or better; swapping two identical silica layers produced zero difference to machine precision.

## Reversed oxide-order experiment

For nominal 50 nm SiO2 and 50 nm Al2O3 layers over Si, a common Au cap strongly screened the layer-order signal. The peak fractional pressure contrast occurred at the shortest tested gap, 100 nm:

| Au cap | peak fractional contrast | delta P at 100 nm |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 nm | 0.0619 | 0.2863 Pa |
| 10 nm | 0.0288 | 0.1464 Pa |
| 20 nm | 0.00858 | 0.04693 Pa |
| 30 nm | 0.00294 | 0.01648 Pa |
| 40 nm | 0.00106 | 0.00598 Pa |
| 60 nm | 0.000144 | 0.000818 Pa |
| 80 nm | 0.000020 | 0.000115 Pa |

At 10 nm Au, the nuisance-orthogonal target fraction was `0.250` with offset, electrostatic `d^-2`, and differential gap-registration terms. Adding ordinary nuisance structure collapsed it: roughness alone to `0.00870`, gain asymmetry to `0.00923`, cap-thickness mismatch to `0.0152`, SiO2-thickness mismatch to `0.0305`, and Al2O3-thickness mismatch to `0.0468`. With the full tested nuisance family, the fraction was about `3.1e-6`.

The corresponding 10%-of-target local tolerance budget at a 100 nm gap was stringent. For a 10 nm Au cap, the adopted model gave approximately `0.135 nm` allowable differential gap mismatch, `0.100 nm` Au-cap mismatch, and `0.292%` relative gain mismatch before one nuisance alone reached 10% of the target amplitude. At 20 nm Au, the gap requirement tightened to about `0.040 nm` and relative gain to `0.086%`.

A geometry search over 20–200 nm oxide thicknesses did not remove the basic problem. Thicker oxides increased raw contrast, but nuisance-orthogonal fractions under even the basic nuisance model remained around 0.21–0.27 for the best 5 nm-cap candidates.

Four-geometry multiplexing at 10 nm Au produced identifiable fraction `0.0195` when nuisance coefficients were globally shared, `0.00213` when gap and gain amplitudes were independent by geometry, and about `2e-6` when all nuisance amplitudes were independent by geometry. Geometry multiplexing therefore helps only when nuisance transfer functions are independently justified as shared.

Adding force-gradient shape as a second observable did not rescue the full free-nuisance model: the identifiable fraction improved only by roughly 12–18%, remaining of order `1e-6`.

## Same-specimen optical-state experiment

A second model experiment used the repository WO3 and oxygen-deficient WO3-x optical parameterizations as a same-specimen state-change proxy. For a 100 nm film on Si against Au, the calculated pressure difference changed sign near 150 nm and reached a fractional state contrast of about `3.59%` at 400 nm. The sign change gives the target more structure than a single inverse power law.

Even so, a completely free nuisance model remained dangerous. The target retained fraction `0.499` against offset plus control-correlated electrostatic response and `0.424` after adding control-correlated gap motion, but only `0.00408` after adding gain and roughness terms and `0.000211` after adding film-thickness modulation.

The same material models predict a large independent optical readout contrast in the far-IR/IR. Using the repository sign convention for complex refractive index, the largest sampled normal-incidence reflectivity difference was about `0.461` near `0.093 eV`. This makes an auxiliary optical state meter plausible as a calibration channel, although it does not by itself prove that the force change has the same origin.

## Matched-null experiment

A matched-null architecture was then simulated by pairing an active state-modulated channel `[t]` with a sham channel `[0]` while forcing nuisance transfer functions to be shared as `[n; n]`.

With six nuisance modes exactly shared between active and null, the target retained `1/sqrt(2) = 0.7071` of its norm. Allowing active/null mismatch sequentially reduced that to `0.353` after electrostatic mismatch, `0.300` after gap-motion mismatch, `0.00462` after gain mismatch, `0.00289` after roughness mismatch, and `0.000211` after film-thickness mismatch.

The matched null is therefore useful only if nuisance transfer equality is itself measured. Relative gain matching is the dominant practical gate in this model.

## Calibrated-nuisance Fisher scenario

Replacing unconstrained nuisance amplitudes with explicit Gaussian calibration priors changes the conclusion quantitatively. With per-point pressure noise `3 mPa`, a control-correlated gap-motion prior of `0.10 nm`, and relative-gain prior of `0.1%`, the adopted linear-Gaussian WO3/WO3-x scenario gives expected local detection significance of about `4.1 sigma` for a full model-state change. Tightening gain calibration to `0.01%` raises this to about `4.3 sigma`; relaxing it to `1%` lowers it to about `2.3 sigma`.

These are scenario results, not apparatus forecasts: the assumed pressure noise and nuisance priors require experimental validation.

## Verdict

The reversed SiO2/Al2O3 layer-order design is falsified as a robust precision-discrimination architecture under the tested nuisance expansion. A common Au cap suppresses the physical target while ordinary gain, roughness, gap, and film mismatches occupy nearly the same measurement-space direction.

The more promising architecture is a reversible same-specimen material-state modulation with a matched sham control, direct gap metrology, in-situ gain calibration, and an independent optical state meter. The next implementation should treat all of these as measured channels and use the repository's canonical evidence/claim workflow rather than encode their uncertainty as undocumented assumptions.
